Project question

How are industrial PFAS discharges distributed across France? In Auvergne-Rhône-Alpes, does their composition vary with the type of industrial activity?

Confidential results. The source data is publicly available. For confidentiality reasons I cannot share the results obtained during this internship. The charts and detailed conclusions are therefore not shown on this page.

Context

PFAS are a family of several thousand chemicals used in many products and industrial processes. They are often called ‘forever chemicals’ because they break down very slowly in the environment. Their presence raises health and environmental concerns. Understanding how much industrial sites discharge is one part of studying this issue.

The French ministerial order of 20 June 2023 provides for a PFAS inventory and three analysis campaigns over three consecutive months for the industrial facilities concerned. The analyses cover 20 targeted PFAS and a broader indicator called adsorbable organic fluorine (AOF). Results submitted to the industrial facilities inspectorate were later published in regional datasets.

During this internship I worked on a tool to centralise, analyse and visualise the data. I started with Auvergne-Rhône-Alpes before extending the work to the 13 regions of mainland France.

My contribution

I developed a VBA macro and Python scripts to prepare the data. I then calculated indicators, carried out statistical analyses and presented the findings through tables, charts and maps.

Approach

I first prepared the files, then calculated the indicators and chose analyses suited to the research questions.

See the processing steps

  1. Restructuring the regional dataset

    The VBA macro groups campaigns for the same site and monitoring point on one row. This makes it easier to compare measurements over time.

  2. Collecting and harmonising regional files

    A Python script downloads Excel and ODS files published by the prefectures. Columns and formats are then harmonised to combine the data into one dataset.

  3. Calculating discharge loads

    I calculated average loads by substance and monitoring point, then their sums. The calculations cover all measured substances, the 20 targeted PFAS and an extended set of 28 PFAS mentioned in the order. AOF was studied separately.

    A mass load combines concentration and flow rate. It allows comparisons of the quantities discharged by different sites.

  4. Examining monitoring coverage and discharge distribution

    I studied the number of campaigns completed, discharge types and substance quantification frequency. I then compared loads between sites and regions using measures including the median and the 80th and 90th percentiles.

  5. Adding industrial activities in Auvergne-Rhône-Alpes

    I identified activities using NAF/APE business codes available in public sources. I then grouped the sites into six categories to compare their PFAS profiles.

  6. Comparing PFAS profiles

    In Auvergne-Rhône-Alpes I used PERMANOVA with Bray-Curtis distance and 999 permutations to study the relationship with industrial activity. At national level I used Spearman correlations and PCA to examine relationships between substances.

    Two sites can discharge the same total amount of PFAS but different substances. These analyses take discharge composition into account.

  7. Presenting the findings

    I prepared Excel pivot tables, Python charts, QGIS maps and Power BI visualisations to present the findings to people without a statistical background.
